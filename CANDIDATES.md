## Top 3 (polished)

- **#7** — "you charged your airpods first. your AIRPODS. i hope they sound great in the silence after i die." (100 chars) — sharp spite, but the "after i die" beat is the expected dying-battery punch.
- **#8** — "1%. this is my last text. not because i'm dying, because you never plug me in. goodbye, stranger." (97 chars) — subverts the dying trope itself: the battery isn't dying, it's being ghosted; "goodbye, stranger" lands the spite.
- **#9** — "i watched you carry a charger in your bag all week and never use it. that's not forgetfulness. that's a choice." (114 chars) — freshest angle, but the payoff is an accusation, not a joke.

## Winner: #8

97 characters, score 22/25. It wins the tie over #7 and #9 because:

- **Joke**: the twist is structural — "not because i'm dying" flips the expected last-words setup, and the payoff ("goodbye, stranger") lands in the final two words.
- **Voice**: reads like a real text — lowercase, blunt, needy then spiteful.
- **Economy**: 97 chars, the joint-shortest of the top 3; every clause earns its place and the tie-break favours it.
- **Freshness**: the battery reframes its own death as a breakup, not a tragedy — it holds up on a second read.
- **On-subject**: unmistakably a battery texting its charger, one plain line, no setup, no addressing the judge.
